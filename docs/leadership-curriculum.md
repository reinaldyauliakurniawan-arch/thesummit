# The Summit v2 — Leadership Curriculum

> **Purpose:** Map every leadership competency to specific game situations. The game functions as a complete leadership curriculum, not a collection of dilemmas.
> **Audience:** Facilitators, HR/L&D professionals, coaches, curriculum designers

---

## Design Philosophy

The Summit teaches leadership the way a flight simulator teaches piloting: through **repeated practice under realistic conditions with safe consequences.** Every competency is taught through a cycle of five stages:

1. **Encounter** — The player faces a situation that requires the competency
2. **Practice** — The player makes a choice (right or wrong)
3. **Evidence** — The system records what the player actually did
4. **Reflection** — The player sees the pattern in their behavior
5. **Transfer** — The player commits to applying the competency in real life

No competency is taught through a single card. Each competency appears across multiple cards at multiple levels, creating a spiraling curriculum where the same skill is practiced in increasingly complex contexts.

## Competency 1: Self-Awareness

### Learning Objective
The player recognizes their default decision-making pattern and its impact on others. They understand that their "natural" leadership style is not the only option available to them.

### Why This Competency First
Every other competency depends on self-awareness. A player who does not know their default pattern cannot deliberately change it. The Basecamp level is designed to surface these defaults before the game creates situations that challenge them.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Self-Awareness Trigger | Level |
|---------|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| BM001 | Deadline ketat: klarifikasi vs eksekusi langsung | Player discovers whether their default is to seek clarity (decisiveness) or to act (risk_taking) | basecamp |
| BM002 | Feedback tajam: terima semua vs diskusi balik | Player discovers whether they default to compliance (empathy) or self-advocacy (decisiveness) | basecamp |
| BM003 | Networking event vs deadline | Player discovers whether they prioritize relationships (long-term) or execution (short-term) | basecamp |
| BM004 | Tugas sulit: pecah jadi milestone vs cari mentor | Player discovers whether they default to self-reliance or seek help (collaboration) | basecamp |
| BM006 | Comfort zone: usulkan proyek vs belajar mandiri | Player discovers whether they default to initiative (decisiveness) or skill-building (adaptability) | basecamp |
| CM002 | Promosi: pertahankan gaya vs tetapkan ekspektasi baru | Player discovers how they handle power transitions — do they default to egalitarian or hierarchical? | camp |
| CM003 | Feedback negatif di retrospective | Player discovers whether they can receive criticism without defensiveness | camp |
| CM004 | Pilih siapa presentasi: siap vs co-presentation | Player discovers whether they default to meritocracy or fairness | camp |
| SM001 | Kelola 3 gaya lead berbeda: forum vs standar minimum | Player discovers whether they default to empowerment (coaching) or control (control) | summit |
| SM002 | Visi 3 tahun: workshop vs susun sendiri | Player discovers whether they default to co-creation (collaboration) or solo analysis (decisiveness) | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **adaptability** signals: Pattern of switching approaches vs rigid default
- **control** signals: Tendency toward authority vs delegation
- **risk_taking** signals: Tendency toward action vs analysis
- Pattern detection: Same-option chosen 5+ consecutive turns → adaptability negative signal

### Reflection Questions
- "What pattern do you notice in your Basecamp choices? Were you consistent, or did you adapt?"
- "When did you choose differently from your default? What made that situation different?"
- "Your profile shows [style]. Does that match how you see yourself as a leader?"
- "Which of your 'strengths' might actually be overused? What happens when you rely on it too much?"

### Real-World Application
- **Challenge:** "In the next week, notice one recurring leadership decision you make on autopilot. Before making it, pause and ask: 'Is this my default, or is this what the situation requires?'"
- **Transfer mechanism:** The player now has vocabulary for their pattern ("I default to control under pressure") and can use this vocabulary in coaching conversations and self-reflection.

## Competency 2: Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

### Learning Objective
The player develops comfort with making significant decisions without complete information. They learn that delaying decisions has its own cost, and that perfect information is never available in real leadership.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Uncertainty Mechanic | Level |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| BM007 | Kesalahan rekan: bicara privat vs lapor langsung | Hidden reveal after choice: "Klien sudah tahu" reframes both options | basecamp |
| BM008 | Perintah tidak etis: ikuti vs sampaikan keberatan | Hidden info before choice: "Atasan sedang diuji" adds context; conditional triggers depend on TT level | basecamp |
| BM009 | Proyek dibatalkan: terima vs minta meeting | No hidden info, but the "correct" choice depends on whether the player has enough TT to survive the confrontation cost | basecamp |
| CM001 | Anggota menurun: fleksibilitas vs tuntut standar | Hidden reveal after choice: burnout context changes meaning of both options | camp |
| CM007 | PHK tidak transparan: tolak vs ikuti | Moral weight: both options have legitimate reasons, creating genuine uncertainty | camp |
| CM008 | Dua anggota berseteru: mediasi vs biarkan | Risk Die roll adds random outcome that the player cannot control | camp |
| CM009 | Target naik 50%: advokat vs motivasi | Conditional triggers: if MP low, advocacy fails; if MP high, motivation works | camp |
| SM005 | Dua lead bersaing: transparan vs pilih diam-diam | Both options have severe costs; hidden conditional on TT level amplifies risk of B | summit |
| SM006 | Keputusan kontroversial: paksa diskusi vs ambil sendiri | All leads watching — social pressure creates uncertainty about political consequences | summit |
| SM007 | Budget dipotong 30%: libatkan lead vs tentukan sendiri | Time pressure: process slower but inclusive vs fast but alienating | summit |
| SM008 | Lead mengambil credit tim: coaching privat vs public shaming | Long-term vs short-term consequence uncertainty | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **decisiveness** signals: Making firm choices (positive) or avoiding decisions (negative)
- **risk_taking** signals: Choosing high-variance options (positive) or always choosing safest option (negative)
- **control** signals: Choosing to control outcomes (positive/negative depending on context)
- Pattern: Consistently choosing safer option during crisis cards → risk_taking negative

### Reflection Questions
- "Which decisions felt the hardest? What made them hard — lack of information, competing values, or fear of consequences?"
- "When you chose the 'safe' option, did you know it was safe, or did it just feel safer?"
- "How did the hidden information change your understanding of the situation? What would you have chosen differently?"
- "In real life, which decisions do you avoid? What would happen if you made them?"

### Real-World Application
- **Challenge:** "Identify one decision you've been postponing because you don't have enough information. Make the best decision you can with 70% of the data. Track what happens."
- **Transfer mechanism:** The game normalizes imperfect decisions. The player learns that 70% information + decisive action often outperforms 95% information + delayed action.

## Competency 3: Empathy & Human-Centered Leadership

### Learning Objective
The player develops the habit of considering other people's needs, emotions, and perspectives — even when it costs them personally. They learn that empathy is not a soft skill but a strategic leadership capability.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Empathy Challenge | Level |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| BM005 | Pujian atasan untuk kerja tim | Credit sharing: prioritize relationship vs honesty | basecamp |
| BM007 | Kesalahan rekan dekat | Friendship vs integrity: protecting someone vs protecting the organization | basecamp |
| CM001 | Anggota tim menurun (burnout) | Performance vs humanity: deadline pressure vs personal suffering | camp |
| CM005 | Anggota berbakat tapi egois | Talent management: accommodate difficult person vs force conformity | camp |
| CM006 | Tim capai target tapi 2-3 orang melakukan semua | Fairness vs efficiency: top performers burning out while others coast | camp |
| CM007 | PHK tidak transparan | Moral courage: refusing to participate in unjust process | camp |
| SM005 | Dua lead bersaing | Fairness: transparent process vs political expediency | summit |
| SM009 | Tim terbaik merusak hubungan tim lain | Values vs results: tolerating toxic high performers | summit |
| SM010 | Sacrifice ambition for team | Personal cost: dream job vs team commitment | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **empathy** signals: Protecting others (positive) vs ignoring needs (negative)
- **collaboration** signals: Prioritizing team outcomes (positive) vs self-interest (negative)
- **coaching** signals: Investing in others' development (positive)
- Pattern: Consistently choosing options with negative cross-player effects → empathy negative

### Reflection Questions
- "When did you choose to protect someone at personal cost? What did that cost feel like?"
- "When did you prioritize efficiency over people? What was the impact on others?"
- "Did you ever choose differently because you imagined how the other person would feel? What happened?"
- "Who in your real work life is the equivalent of the 'struggling team member'? How do you usually handle them?"

### Real-World Application
- **Challenge:** "Have a 15-minute conversation with a colleague focused entirely on their experience, not their output. Ask: 'What's the hardest part of your work right now?' Just listen."
- **Transfer mechanism:** The game creates an emotional memory of choosing empathy over efficiency. The player can recall this memory when facing real situations.

## Competency 4: Team Development & Coaching

### Learning Objective
The player learns to invest in others' growth as a core leadership responsibility, not as a distraction from "real work." They understand that building others' capability is the highest-leverage leadership activity.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Coaching Challenge | Level |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| BS007 | Sistem downtime: delegasikan vs ambil alih | Delegation as coaching vs control | basecamp |
| BS017 | Estimasi waktu meleset: akui vs kerja lembur | Teaching the team to plan vs heroic individual effort | basecamp |
| CM005 | Anggota berbakat tapi egois | Design collaboration opportunities vs direct feedback | camp |
| CM006 | 2-3 orang melakukan semua kerja | Mentoring program vs task rotation | camp |
| CS001 | Distribusi tugas membosankan | Fair rotation vs efficiency assignment | camp |
| CS005 | Hiring budget terbatas | Growth mindset vs experience | camp |
| CS006 | Onboarding tidak efektif | Buddy system vs self-learning | camp |
| SM003 | Promosi bintang tapi tim kehilangan momentum | Succession planning: prepare successor first | summit |
| SM004 | Perubahan strategi besar | Breakdown strategy into team-executable initiatives | summit |
| SS001 | Ukur efektivitas 5 tim berbeda | Fair measurement framework vs standardized OKR | summit |
| SS002 | Budget pelatihan terbatas | Pooled collective learning vs proportional allocation | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **coaching** signals: Investing in others' growth (positive) vs neglecting development (negative)
- **collaboration** signals: Building team capability (positive) vs individual execution (negative)
- Pattern: Choosing "do it myself" options consistently → coaching negative

### Reflection Questions
- "How many times did you choose to develop someone else vs doing it yourself? What's the ratio?"
- "When you delegated, was it because you trusted the person, or because you didn't want to do the work yourself?"
- "Who in your real team is ready for the next level? What have you done to prepare them?"
- "Your profile shows [coaching score]. Is this aligned with how you actually lead?"

### Real-World Application
- **Challenge:** "Identify one team member who could grow with the right opportunity. Create that opportunity this week — assign them a stretch task, introduce them to a senior stakeholder, or recommend them for a visible project."
- **Transfer mechanism:** The game creates tangible experience of "investing in others and seeing the return." The player has felt the TT gain from coaching choices.

## Competency 5: Conflict Navigation

### Learning Objective
The player develops the ability to engage with conflict productively rather than avoiding it or escalating it. They learn that conflict is not inherently negative — unaddressed conflict is more destructive than well-managed conflict.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Conflict Challenge | Level |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| BM009 | Proyek dibatalkan: terima vs minta meeting | Constructive dissent: challenge decisions vs comply | basecamp |
| BS019 | Dua rekan konflik teknis | Mediation vs taking sides | basecamp |
| CM008 | Dua anggota berseteru terbuka | Stop debate vs let them resolve | camp |
| CM010 | Senior menolak proses baru | Inclusion vs authority: listen vs enforce | camp |
| CS007 | Bug kritis dari kode senior | Blameless post-mortem vs accountability | camp |
| CS008 | Dua subtim konflik arsitektur | Facilitation vs unilateral decision | camp |
| SM006 | Keputusan kontroversial semua diam | Force discussion vs decide alone | summit |
| SM008 | Lead mengambil credit tim | Private coaching vs public confrontation | summit |
| SS003 | Framework baru dengan skeptisisme | Pilot gradual vs force rollout | summit |
| SS004 | Presentasi kinerja campuran ke board | Transparent vs spin | summit |
| SS006 | Debat arsitektur menyerang personal | Mediation vs authoritarian resolution | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **collaboration** signals: Mediating (positive) vs escalating (negative)
- **decisiveness** signals: Addressing conflict directly (positive) vs avoiding (negative)
- **empathy** signals: Understanding both sides (positive) vs dismissing (negative)

### Reflection Questions
- "When conflict arose, did you address it or avoid it? What was the cost of your choice?"
- "Did you take sides, or did you try to understand both perspectives?"
- "In your real work, which conflicts are you currently avoiding? What's the cost of that avoidance?"
- "How does your team handle disagreement? Is it productive or destructive?"

### Real-World Application
- **Challenge:** "Identify one unresolved conflict in your team. This week, initiate a conversation about it. Use the framework: understand both perspectives, acknowledge the tension, propose a path forward."
- **Transfer mechanism:** The game provides practice in a safe environment. The player has experienced both the cost of avoiding conflict and the benefit of addressing it.

## Competency 6: Accountability & Integrity

### Learning Objective
The player learns to take responsibility for outcomes — including failures — and to act with integrity when it costs them personally. They understand that accountability is not about blame, but about ownership.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Accountability Challenge | Level |
|---------|-----------|------------------------|-------|
| BM007 | Kesalahan rekan dekat | Loyalty vs integrity | basecamp |
| BM008 | Perintah tidak etis | Compliance vs moral courage | basecamp |
| BM010 | Menyalahkan kondisi eksternal | Owning results vs explaining context | basecamp |
| CM007 | PHK tidak transparan | Refusing to participate vs following orders | camp |
| CM009 | Target naik 50% | Advocating for team vs managing expectations | camp |
| CS007 | Bug kritis dari kode senior | Blameless culture vs accountability culture | camp |
| CS008 | Dua subtim konflik arsitektur | Owning the resolution vs blaming | camp |
| SM005 | Dua lead bersaing | Transparent process vs political control | summit |
| SM009 | Tim terbaik merusak hubungan tim lain | Values enforcement vs results tolerance | summit |
| SS005 | Tim laporkan metrik dipoles | Systemic fix vs punishment | summit |
| SS006 | Metrik dipoles selama 6 bulan | Cultural correction vs individual blame | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **decisiveness** signals: Taking ownership (positive) vs deflecting (negative)
- **empathy** signals: Protecting others from unfair blame (positive)
- **collaboration** signals: Shared accountability (positive) vs individual blame (negative)

### Reflection Questions
- "When did you take responsibility for a negative outcome? When did you deflect?"
- "Have you ever been in a situation like BM008 in real life? What did you do?"

- **Transfer mechanism:** The game demonstrates that accountability is rewarded (reputation +1, TT +1) while blame-shifting is punished (reputation -1, TT -2).

## Competency 7: Strategic Thinking

### Learning Objective
The player develops the ability to think beyond the current turn — considering delayed consequences, accumulated effects, and long-term positioning. They learn that strategic leadership requires sacrificing short-term gains for long-term outcomes.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Strategic Challenge | Level |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| BM001 | Deadline ketat | Scope clarification (delayed benefit via schedule_event) vs immediate execution | basecamp |
| BM004 | Tugas sulit | Seek mentor (long-term benefit) vs self-execute (short-term gain) | basecamp |
| BS016 | Sertifikasi profesional | Long-term investment (certification) vs immediate skill (project) | basecamp |
| CM004 | Pilih presenter | Develop both people vs optimize for one | camp |
| CS001 | Distribusi tugas membosankan | Build sustainable team culture vs short-term efficiency | camp |
| CS005 | Hiring budget terbatas | Invest in potential vs wait for perfect candidate | camp |
| SM001 | Kelola 3 gaya lead berbeda | Build learning culture vs set minimum standards | summit |
| SM002 | Visi 3 tahun | Co-create vision (buy-in) vs solo analysis (depth) | summit |
| SM007 | Budget dipotong 30% | Empower lead to reprioritize (sustainable) vs dictate cuts (efficient) | summit |
| SS001 | Ukur 5 tim berbeda | Invest in fair framework (complex) vs standard OKR (fast) | summit |
| SS003 | Adopsi framework baru | Pilot gradual (proven) vs rollout sekaligus (fast but risky) | summit |
| SS007 | 3/5 tim untuk politics | Eliminate bureaucracy (strategic) vs new KPIs (tactical) | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **adaptability** signals: Flexible planning (positive) vs rigid approach (negative)
- **control** signals: Long-term positioning (positive) vs short-term control (negative)
- Pattern: Choosing options with `schedule_event` effects → strategic thinking evidence

### Reflection Questions
- "How many of your choices considered what would happen 3 turns later? How many only considered the current turn?"
- "When did a delayed consequence surprise you? Were you prepared for it?"
- "In your real work, are you making decisions for this quarter or for next year?"
- "What would you do differently if you knew every decision would be evaluated 6 months later?"

### Real-World Application
- **Challenge:** "Identify one decision you're making this week. Ask: 'What are the consequences of this decision 6 months from now? 1 year from now?' Write down your answer before deciding."
- **Transfer mechanism:** The game's delayed consequence system creates visceral experience of "decisions having future costs." The player can recall this feeling when making real strategic decisions.

## Competency 8: Change Leadership

### Learning Objective
The player develops the ability to lead others through change — including resistance, skepticism, and fear. They learn that change leadership requires patience, inclusion, and adaptability rather than authority and speed.

### Cards That Teach It

| Card ID | Situation | Change Challenge | Level |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| BS012 | Belajar tools baru: training resmi vs mandiri | Structured vs self-directed learning adoption | basecamp |
| BS014 | Dokumentasi berantakan | Rewrite from scratch vs fix critical parts first | basecamp |
| BS020 | Ambil alih proyek tanpa dokumentasi | Audit dulu vs langsung kerja | basecamp |
| CM009 | Target naik 50% | Advocate for team vs toxic positivity | camp |
| CM010 | Senior menolak proses baru | Include skeptic in design vs enforce | camp |
| CS009 | Klien minta perubahan mid-sprint | Negotiate scope vs accept and rescope | camp |
| SM004 | Perubahan strategi organisasi | Breakdown strategy vs distribute document | summit |
| SS003 | Adopsi framework baru | Pilot gradual vs force rollout | summit |
| SS007 | Bureaucracy politics | Eliminate non-value activities vs new KPI | summit |
| SS008 | Merger budaya berbeda | Co-create new culture vs impose existing | summit |

### Evidence Generated
- **adaptability** signals: Flexible change approach (positive) vs rigid enforcement (negative)
- **collaboration** signals: Inclusive change (positive) vs top-down change (negative)
- **coaching** signals: Developing change capability in others (positive)

### Reflection Questions
- "When you faced resistance to change, did you include the resisters or override them?"
- "What does 'successful change' look like — getting to the destination, or bringing people along?"

- **Challenge:** "For the next change initiative you lead, identify the 3 most skeptical stakeholders. Before announcing the change, have a private conversation with each of them. Ask: 'What concerns you about this?' Listen before you explain."
- **Transfer mechanism:** The game demonstrates that inclusive change (higher TT, positive cross-player effects) produces better outcomes than forced change (lower TT, negative cross-player effects).

## Curriculum Sequence

The competencies are not taught in isolation. They spiral across levels:

```
BASECAMP: Self-Awareness → Decision-Making → Empathy → Accountability → Strategic Thinking
    ↓ (player establishes patterns)
CAMP: Conflict Navigation → Team Development → Change Leadership → Empathy (deeper)
    ↓ (player's patterns are tested)
SUMMIT: All competencies at organizational scale → Emotional peak → Reflection → Transfer
    ↓
REAL WORLD: One concrete action based on blind spots
```

### Level Progression Logic

| Level | Competency Focus | Why |
|-------|-----------------|-----|
| **Basecamp** | Self-Awareness, Decision-Making | Must understand own patterns before leading others. Simple dilemmas reveal defaults. |
| **Camp** | Team Development, Conflict, Change | Now leading others. Competencies shift from internal to interpersonal. |
| **Summit** | Strategic Thinking, Integrity at Scale | Organizational leadership. Higher stakes, less control, more ambiguity. |
| **Post-Game** | Transfer | Apply what was learned. The curriculum is incomplete without this step. |
